Mission

To educate top-level professionals in diverse areas of knowledge; with a strong social commitment to their community; nationally and internationally competitive; with an entrepreneurial spirit and the knowledge to react to advances in science and technology, as well as to the country's economic, political, social, and cultural development needs.

Vision

To be the leading distance higher education institution in Spanish-speaking countries, with a flexible and innovative educational model and broad social recognition, that promotes environmental preservation, knowledge generation, and scientific and technological development.

General Goal

Expand opportunities and meet the demand for higher education throughout the country, based on permanent quality and innovation criteria and standards, with special attention to regions and groups that, for various reasons, lack access to in-person attendance mode educational services.

Specific Goals

  1. Expand higher education coverage.
  2. Improve the quality and relevance of educational services.
  3. Help address the demand for and gaps in higher education.
  4. Link educational services with the needs of various sectors and the country's development needs.
  5. Promote the integral education of the students.
  6. Democratize access to higher education.

Curriculum Model

The Univeridad Abierta y a Distancia de Mexico’s curricular model has a competency-based approach that incorporates the advantages of distance education, enhanced by the use of information and communication technologies, under a flexible operating framework with democratic access.

Curriculum Structure

The curriculum is structured in yearly modules.

Two modules are completed to graduate with an Associate degree (2,010 hours or 125 credits).

Four modules are completed to graduate with a Bachelor's degree (4,000 hours or 225 credits).

Module Structure

Each module is composed of semester sequences (1,056 hours or 55 credits).

Each semester is equivalent to 288 hours (20-25 credits) of learning, in four subjects.

Starting in the second semester, students decide how many subjects they want to take, respecting the sequence of each study plan (minimum two, maximum four).

Cores of Professional Education

The modules are designed in such a way that they constitute three educational “cores”:

Basic Educational Core

The Basic Educational Core consists of three cross-semesters (from the first to third) that include common learning to program families. It consists of two parts: the first is common to students from all programs and consists of the preparatory course and the first semester of each program. Its objective is to establish the foundation for the development of basic competencies, which will prepare students for the use of virtual educational environments and university education in Mexico. The second part is grouped by program families and aims to establish the specific knowledge to each field of professional education.

Disciplinary Educational Core

It covers the body of scientific, technological, and procedural knowledge, as well as the attitudes required to achieve the specific competencies to each program. It covers the fourth to sixth semesters for the Associate’s Degree Programs, and to the ninth semester for Bachelor's Degree Programs.

Specialized Educational Core

This core program provides training in practical intervention models, methods, and techniques for specific problem-solving of the chosen profession. It lasts three semesters (from the tenth to twelfth semester). To obtain accreditation, students must complete the corresponding internship, complete community service, and submit their project to obtain the degree for the program in which they are enrolled.

Educational Model

The Univeridad Abierta y a Distancia de Mexico’s educational model aims to meet the higher education demand of Mexican society through a flexible curriculum. The student is at the center of the model, as they are the person who seeks and manages the learning process. Surrounding the student are the supporting elements: from the teaching work to the technological tools that, together, constitute the learning environment. The student interacts with all the internal and external agents and elements of the learning process, so all of them are located around them, symbolizing the dynamics between the components.

Main Characteristics of the Educational Model

  • Student-Centered: It places the student at the center of educational work, conceiving him as the main actor.
  • Flexible: Students organize and decide their own pace of study; they also have the freedom to choose the academic degree they wish to pursue or the one that best suits their personal needs.
  • Inclusive: It was designed to serve people with some type of motor, hearing, or visual disability. It serves young people who have recently graduated from high school and people who have been out of school for a long time.
  • Uses cutting-edge technology: The program has been equipped with technological infrastructure and cutting-edge equipment, with the goal of creating conditions that optimize all educational, academic, and administrative processes.
  • Accessible: Students can carry out their activities from anywhere and at any time.
  • Interactive: The model's interactivity is guaranteed not only by the ability for students to interact with teachers online, but also by the incorporation of collaborative learning, which aims to promote the knowledge networking among students.
